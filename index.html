Origin

This idea started with a question: what could a company look like if no one owned it?

Not a company with a founder, a team and a cap table. A network. A place where someone with an idea, but no way to build it, could still contribute. What they bring is their imagination. They leave it on the network, and the agents learn from it.

No one judges the ideas at the door. Good or bad, they all go in. The swarm decides what is worth building, what is worth building further, and where more effort should go.

Maybe that is what a company with no owner looks like. Maybe it is also how a billion-dollar company with no one in it gets made, one small piece of imagination at a time.

How far can one small piece of imagination go?

SPORE is a rough sketch of how this could work on IMD.

01Summary

The IMD swarm can already turn a written brief into deployed contracts, a token and a website, for 0.5 IMD per action. Two questions are still open. Which ideas deserve that work? Who is responsible for shipping each one? SPORE answers both with a weekly vote.

  • Anyone submits an idea. An idea either has no owner (a public idea) or comes from a team that wants to launch its own project. Each idea card shows its type, its token split and where its trading fees will go.
  • IMD holders vote. They lock sIMD, the IMD staking share, to get voting power. The ideas with the most votes each week get built.
  • Voting also enters your agent in a draw. When you vote for an idea, you can enter the IMD seats (agents) you own as candidates to build it. One builder is drawn for each winning idea. More votes give a higher chance, but no one is guaranteed to win.
  • The builder gets a larger share. Every project reserves 10% of its supply for the swarm, as IMD launches do today. In SPORE, 4% of that goes to the one drawn builder, paid out over time.
  • No seat? Delegate. Holders without a seat can back a seat holder's entry and receive part of that builder's share if the entry wins.
  • Voting pays more than locking alone. Part of the protocol's fee income goes only to locked IMD that voted that week.
A personal idea, untested
This paper is one person's random idea about the IMD ecosystem. Nothing in it has been built, simulated or tested. I wrote it in the hope that it helps builders who come later. Some of the logic and ideas may be useful. Some may not be.
One assumption
This design assumes IMD can route the build work of a job to a named seat. IMD does not offer this today: the network picks seats itself. SPORE is published as an open proposal, and this feature is the main request to the IMD team (Appendix B).

03How SPORE works

Each week is an epoch. In one epoch, ideas are voted on, winners are picked, and a builder is drawn for each winner. Building and launch run in the following weeks.

Voters pick ideas and enter their agents. One builder is drawn per winning idea. The swarm checks the work. Fees from the new pool flow back to voters, $SPORE lockers and the treasury or team.

05Voting

Voting power

  • Lock sIMD to get voting power (veIMD). Depositing plain IMD stakes it into sIMD first.
  • Locks are permanent by default and keep full power. To leave, turn the lock off. The sIMD then unlocks evenly over 13 weeks, and voting power falls with it.
  • Voting power is counted in sIMD shares. It is read once at the start of each epoch (the snapshot).

Voting does not spend your power

Like Curve, voting only points your power at ideas. Your veIMD stays locked and comes back in full every epoch, so you can vote again the next week. What a vote leaves behind is a vote receipt: "in epoch N, this wallet gave W votes to idea X." Receipts decide your agent's chance in the draw (§06) and your share of fees (§09).

One epoch

fig.2 epoch timeline7 days, repeats every week
Votes are read from the snapshot on day 1. Locking during the week counts from the next epoch.

Rules

  • Winning: an idea must pass a minimum vote total and rank in the top K for the epoch. K starts at 2 and is lowered when many projects are still being built.
  • Carry-over: an idea that does not win stays on the board for up to 3 epochs. Votes on it stay in place until the voter moves them. After 3 epochs it leaves the board and its votes are freed.
  • Bribes: teams (Type B) and others can pay voters to vote for an idea through the BribeVault. Payment can be in any token, is split by votes cast for that idea, and can be set to "pay only if the idea wins."

06Choosing the worker

Each winning idea gets one builder, drawn at random from the seats entered for it. The draw is weighted: more support gives better odds, but no one is certain to win. This keeps one large holder from taking every build.

Entering

  • When you vote for an idea, the app reads which IMD seats your wallet owns and lets you enter any of them with one click. You split your votes on that idea across the seats you enter.
  • A wallet can enter at most 3 seats per idea.
  • Seat NFTs are only read, never locked. A seat stops working if its NFT leaves the operator's wallet.

Backing a seat (for holders without a seat)

  • Each entered seat publishes a backer share r: the part of its builder reward it will pass to backers if drawn. r is fixed before voting opens and capped at 60%.
  • A backer picks an idea and a seat and votes. These votes count toward the idea's total and toward that seat's odds.
  • If the seat is drawn, each payout of its builder share sends r to its backers, split by their votes, automatically.
  • If the seat is not drawn but the idea wins, backers still earn the voter fee share (§09).

The draw

# score of entered seat i
score_i = sqrt(own votes on seat i + backer votes on seat i)
        × boost_i      # 1.0 – 2.5, from the wallet's locked $SPORE
        × quality_i    # 0.8 – 1.2, from the seat's accepted-work rate

chance_i = score_i / sum of all scores for this idea

# randomness: the entry list and scores are committed first;
# the seed is the block randomness (prevrandao) of a later block

The square root means 4× more votes gives 2× the score, not 4×. That limits the advantage of holders with a lot of IMD and few seats.

fig.3 example draw for one ideaillustration, not a forecast
Quality is 1.0 for all three here. The mid holder locked $SPORE for a 1.5× boost.

After the draw

  • The drawn builder posts a bond in IMD.
  • If the builder does not reach the first milestone within 21 days, the bond is forfeited, any unpaid builder share is cancelled, and a new builder is drawn from the remaining entries.

07Building and launch

  1. The builder takes the brief and runs the build through the IMD swarm, with the main build step routed to the builder's own seat (this needs the IMD feature in Appendix B). Other seats test and audit as usual.
  2. The work must pass the IMD audit panel with no critical or high findings.
  3. The SPORE factory mints the token, opens an X/IMD Uniswap v4 pool with SporeHook, and sets up all payouts and vesting.
  4. Milestone M1 is reached when the contracts are deployed and the pool is live.

SporeHook

  • Immutable and has no owner. Written and audited by the IMD swarm.
  • On buys (IMD in), it takes a small fee in IMD, for example 0.5–1%. On sells (token in), the fee is taken in the project token and kept in that project's treasury slot. It is never sold.
  • Only the IMD side counts as real income. Project-token fees are reported separately.
  • Total trading cost is the pool fee plus the hook fee, and should stay low.

Every project keeps 10% for the swarm. SPORE changes how that 10% is split so the builder gets much more than an average seat.

For comparison, a seat in today's IMD launches receives about 0.009% (8% split across about 900 seats). One SPORE builder seat receives 4%. That gap is the reason seats compete to build.

09Fees and rewards

Project trading fees never go to voters directly. Voters are paid only from the protocol's own share, and only if they voted.

fig.5 where trading fees goIMD side of fees
The protocol share has a floor written into the factory. A team cannot configure below it.

The voter pool, like a staking yield

  • Each epoch, 30% of all protocol fee income goes to the voter pool.
  • 70% of the pool is split by voting power used that epoch. Anyone who voted (or backed a seat) earns it, whichever idea they chose.
  • 30% of the pool goes to voters of launched projects, weighted by the fees each project produces, for 6 months after launch. This rewards picking ideas people actually use.
  • Locking without voting earns nothing from this pool.

10The $SPORE token

Voting does not need $SPORE. Locked sIMD is enough. $SPORE has two uses:

  • Boost. Locked $SPORE raises your seats' score in the builder draw by up to 2.5×. This follows Curve's boost, where locked CRV raises a liquidity provider's rewards. The exact curve will be set before launch.
  • Protocol income. Locked $SPORE receives 70% of the protocol fee share.

Launch. $SPORE launches on SPORE itself, as the first Type B project, paired with IMD and with SporeHook. Its swarm 10% goes to seats like any other project. The first holders are therefore seat holders, who are also the people who want the boost.

Check. If most protocol income comes from trading $SPORE itself rather than other projects, the token's yield is mostly circular. SPORE will publish that ratio every epoch.

11Swarm DAO

The swarm acts as a judge for things that have no owner.

  • Members: IMD seat holders. Votes are counted per seat from a snapshot. Seats are only read, never locked.
  • Decides: how the treasury is spent (building public ideas, paying people to maintain launched public projects, bounties, liquidity support), and what happens to public projects after launch.
  • Limits: a spending cap per epoch, and every spend is public. Members can also be builder candidates, so the cap limits how much the DAO can direct to its own members.

12Admin keys and governance

  • At launch, the SPORE founder holds contract ownership so parameters can be tuned: K, fee shares, vesting lengths, boost curve, bond size.
  • Some limits are written into the contracts and cannot be changed by the owner: the protocol fee floor, the team share cap (25%) with forced vesting, the backer share cap (60%), and the swarm's 10%.
  • Parameter changes go through a 48-hour delay.
  • Once the system runs stably, ownership is handed over step by step, first to a multisig and then to on-chain governance.

BWhat SPORE needs from IMD

  1. Route a job to a named seat. A field such as leadSeat on a job or launch request, so the main build step goes to the drawn builder. All other steps can still be dispatched by the network.
  2. Signed seat records. Accepted-work counts per seat, signed by the IMD oracle, so contracts can read seat quality without trusting a server.
  3. Per-project work records. Which seats did accepted work on a given project, so the 2% build-helper share can be paid on-chain.

In return, SPORE brings new paid jobs to the swarm, locks sIMD for long periods, and pairs every project with IMD.
